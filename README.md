# Doberega — тиша та спокій

A small, dependency-free canvas prototype for a calm lake scene rendered as a regular ASCII character field. The screen grid and distant forest glyphs are generated on resize or when character spacing changes. Water animation is a separate mathematical model, so the same wave sampling can later feed another renderer.

The water uses the common 2:1 game projection, with axes at about 26.6°; touch ripples use the inverse projection so they stay anchored under the pointer. Its calm breeze model layers slow, nearly parallel swells with faint crosswind variations. Coherent wave light maps to a fixed ASCII density ramp, from sparse punctuation to dense symbols, using one editable, muted blue-green water color. The scene is set at golden hour from a seat at the water's edge. A low sun glows above three hazy mountain ridges; slow value-noise clouds drift across the sky, the first stars twinkle overhead, and a small flock of birds crosses now and then. The far shore has three overlapping forest depths (pine, spruce, oak, and rounded crowns) lit on the side facing the sun, with gentle deterministic canopy sway. The water mirrors the shore, the reflection wobbling with waves and ripples, and a path of sun glints widens toward the viewer. Fish occasionally break the surface. In the foreground, reeds and cattails sway at the edges of the frame, beside a rock and a few floating lily pads.

## Language

English and Ukrainian, switched in **Settings** on the main menu. English is the default; Ukrainian is chosen automatically when the system language is Ukrainian or Russian. A manual choice is remembered. All text — UI, fish, bait, rods, spots and their traits, the handbook and messages — lives in `src/i18n.js`; static markup uses `data-i18n` / `data-i18n-aria` attributes.

## Fishing spots

Each spot has its own ASCII landscape, built from a profile in `src/scene.js` (horizon, ridges, forest, near banks, structures, water behaviour, foreground):

- **Ставок за селом** — a pond in open farmland: big sky, a low flat far bank of green meadow and a ploughed strip, scattered round trees and tall poplars, a few small cottages in the distance, a reed fringe along the far waterline, thick reeds and cattails on the near right, and on the near left a big old oak on our own bank: its trunk rises from the corner, a long limb reaches over the water and the crown fills the top-left corner (green in summer, rust and gold in autumn). Its leaves let go now and then, tumble down on the wind, settle on the water with a faint ring, float a while and sink; in autumn they fall about seven times as often, and more in the rain. On a tall phone screen the tree is laid out taller so the crown still comes down toward the water.
- **Тихе озеро** — an old pine forest along the far shore with low blue hills beyond it. A wooden fire lookout tower rises above the treetops (it is painted under the forest silhouette, so only its top shows; its cabin window is dark glass by day and a warm lamp after dark). In a clearing on the bank a log cabin smokes from its chimney, its windows lit at night; a wooden jetty runs out from it toward us on posts, and a rowing boat tied up alongside rocks gently on the water. Reeds, a boulder, a few lily pads and a grassy bank at our feet.
- **Замкове озеро** — after a photo of an alpine lake: a dark wooded hill of distinct tree crowns rises on the left to a sheer limestone cliff topped by an ancient ruined castle (broken grey walls, gutted towers, ivy, no roofs); a snowy alpine massif fills the right, built from glyph cells in a flat palette: `/` and `\` follow the slope, faces facing the sun are lighter, snow runs down the gullies and leaves steep rock bare, woods cover the lower slopes, with blue distance haze; an arched stone bridge sits under the cliff and two red-roofed houses on the far shore. Calm emerald water.
- **Прип'ять** — the cooling pond of the Chornobyl plant, seen across flat water: the whole plant stretches along the far shore over a low line of yellowing willows (the tall, broad unfinished cooling tower on the left with a crane beside it, units 5 and 6 under tower cranes, a lone steel gantry, the arch of the New Safe Confinement, the long main building with two red-and-white stacks whose beacons blink after dusk). Dry yellow and brown woods, slightly murky grey-green water, a big rusty half-sunk river freighter near the far bank, bow out of the water: riveted plates with seams, a row of portholes weeping rust, patches of old green paint, weed at the waterline, the hawse hole with its chain and draft marks at the bow, a broken railing and a cargo hatch on deck, the deckhouse and wheelhouse with their windows gone, a leaning funnel with a faded red band and a mast with slack stays, all over a dark hull so it reads as one shape (after dark its plates glow a faint, breathing green), dry straw-coloured reeds framing the view, and a rusty radiation warning sign, drawn in glyphs like the rocks, leaning among them on the right.
- **Старий міст** — fishing from a wooden bridge over a fast river: we look downstream past the railing, willow-lined banks recede and the river swings right round a bend, leaving the view past the right edge; the current runs away from us and turns with the bend in long streaks and flecks of foam (no casting onto the railing).

Switching spots in the menu rebuilds the scene behind it.

The far landscape keeps moving too (`src/ambient-engine.js`, redrawn every frame on top of the static layer for under a millisecond): wind runs through the meadow by the pond and the grassy banks at the old bridge in bright travelling gusts, smoke rises from cottage chimneys and drifts downwind (flattened by rain), mist creeps along the far shore — thick at dawn and dusk, light at night, and never in rain or over the fast river at the old bridge — the leaves on the castle hill stir, the near trees on the old bridge's banks sway, thin cloud clings to the alpine flanks at the castle lake, and the plant's stacks at Pripyat blink red after dusk.

## Colour grade

The scene bakes a colour grade into its own colours (`GRADE` in `src/theme.js`): the land layer is graded on the GPU as it is drawn (the same grade as a 3×3 matrix, `GRADE_MATRIX`), and woods, grass, water, reflections and the sky take graded colours, while the sun, its glints, lit windows and night lights keep their warmth. It is a soft, cool evening grade, muted colour shifted toward slate blue at full brightness, with a soft vignette at the edges.

## Mystic nights

Deep in the night strange things happen (`src/night-events.js`): will-o'-the-wisps drift over the far water, now and then an apparition by the far shore, one of four picked at random (a boat with no oars and a lantern on its bow, its hooded boatman standing still, drifting along the far water into the mist; a ghostly angler who sits on the far bank, makes one cast to a faintly green float and dissolves; a mavka, a pale girl with long hair, who walks into the water and leaves rings; or just a lantern's light walking along the far shore, stopping, swaying and guttering out), and two red eyes surface near the bank before slipping under in rings. Each event is announced as a `doberega:night` window event (`detail.type`: figure with `detail.kind` boat / angler / mavka / lantern, mavkaSink, eyes, eyesSink), ready for sounds later.

## Season

**Налаштування → Сезон** picks spring, summer, autumn, winter or auto (`currentSeason()` in `src/theme.js`; auto follows the calendar: spring from March, summer from June, autumn from September, winter from December). It changes only the look of the place, except that the burbot bites on autumn nights.

- **Spring:** broadleaf crowns in fresh light greens, about one tree in five in white or pink blossom (`SPRING` in `src/forest-engine.js`), bright young grass with twice the flowers, in white, yellow, violet and orange, the pond's oak in new leaf and green reeds; Pripyat's woods turn green too.
- **Summer:** green woods and grass.
- **Autumn:** broadleaf crowns turn into heavy masses of `0 O @` in blood red, rust, gold and brown (`AUTUMN`; conifers stay green), the grass yellow, and the oak's leaves fall seven times as often.
- **Small life:** an idle float sends out a faint ring every few seconds, and a strike flings up a burst of spray (`splash`); bubbles break the surface in little chains, most at the pond and Pripyat (`drawBubbles`, `water.bubbles`); birds cross the sky by the season (`birdsAt`: geese south in autumn and back in spring, swallows in summer, crows in winter), a bird starts out of the woods now and then and gusts shake snow or leaves off the trees (`spawnLife`), and in winter clumps of snow off the oak; falling stars on clear nights, far more in August (`drawMeteor`); fireflies by the season (full in summer); bats at dusk and before dawn (`updateBats`); cottage windows flicker like a stove; under a hard pull the rod tip quivers and the line hums and sings.
- **Glyphs only:** nothing solid shows under the glyphs. The flat parts of buildings (`paintStructures`) are laid on a dark ground as a dense field of fine characters in their own colour, wherever their detail glyphs leave room; the alpine ranges and the far hills are stippled densely over a darkened ground; the wreck's hull, the radiation sign's paint and the shore ice are dark between their glyphs; floes are slabs of glyphs on the water's grid; birds and beasts are outlined glyphs.
- **Winter:** snow over the painted land (`snowImageData` in `src/theme.js`, run once per rebuild before the water is painted): meadows, hills, banks and roofs go white in the light and blue-grey in the shade, while warm walls, pale stone and plain grey rock take only a dusting and lamplight and the water are left alone. Broadleaf trees are bare, a sparse web of twigs with snow on the upper ones (`bareGlyph`), the near willows a grey-brown haze; conifers carry snow along each tier; the woods on the castle hill are heavy with snow, white on the upper crowns over cold blue shade (blue, so the snow pass leaves it), and hold still; the bank we stand on is snow with dry straw poking through, the reeds are dry with frosted tips, the oak bare with no leaves to fall, and there are no fireflies. Showers fall as snow: slow flakes drifting and swaying, no splashes, no rain sound, no thunder or lightning (the bite still livens as in rain). The water goes a cold steel grey (`seasonWater`, `seasonDeep`) under a paler sky, and stays open, but a ragged rim of shore ice (`iceAt` in `src/canvas-renderer.js`) runs along the far shore, our bank and the river banks: nothing can be cast onto it, and ducks, splashes and the school keep off it. A few floes drift on the current or slowly before the wind (`moveFloes`, drawn clipped to the water), the open water steams on frosty mornings (`fillSteam`), there are no lily pads, the roofs are under snow with icicles along the eaves and, except in February, holiday lights that twinkle after dark, the wreck at Pripyat is iced over (`iceOver`: snow only on what faces up, patches of rime over the rust, icicles down the hull and a collar of ice at the waterline), and on winter days bullfinches call in to sit on the oak's boughs, the cattails or the bridge rail (`updateFinches` in `src/wildlife.js`). The summer birds go quiet: only a tit and a bullfinch call, and there are no crickets, tree frogs or cows.

## Fish pictures

Fish in the catch window, the handbook and the trophy cabinet are detailed portraits (`src/fishing/fish-portrait.js`), drawn the same way as the social cards in `tools/cards` but in each species' own colours from `FISH_LOOKS`: a rounded, shaded body with its markings, rayed fins, a ringed eye with a glint and a dark outline, shaded in dithered steps.

Instagram posts (`tools/posts`, open `/tools/posts/` on the dev server, saved through `tools/posts/save.py` into `posts/`) put real frames of the game (`tools/posts/shots`, taken from the running game with `?debug=1&time=…`) together with the game's fish and rod sprites, its palette and the Handjet font: an intro, the five waters, the fish, the rods, the sunsets, a storm and the night. Captions are in `posts/captions.md`; `?lang=en` makes the English set with its captions in `posts/en/`.

## Grass and sparkle

Grass on the river banks and on the bank we stand on (pond and lake, `nearBank` in `src/scene.js`) is drawn by `src/grass-engine.js` as single strokes: every blade is a `|` glyph turned to its own angle (the glyph batch carries a rotation per glyph), near blades are two or three segments that bend further toward the tip, and gusts sweep across the field as travelling bands. Dark earth shows between the blades. Flowers dot the grass by day, dew sparkles at sunrise, and fireflies pulse over the grass and the shore at night.

Trees on the river banks have crowns of separate clumps of leaves (`bankTreeGlyph` in `src/forest-engine.js`), each lit on the sun's side and dark underneath, over a trunk that forks into branches; grass keeps out of the crowns.

Water has depth (`shallowAt` in `src/canvas-renderer.js`): shallow and lighter, with more of the spot's own colour, toward our bank and beside river banks, darker out over the depths, as one smooth gradient. The castle lake is glacial turquoise.

The bank we stand on fades into the water over a few soft steps.

## Time of day

A full in-game day takes 21 min 20 s of real time and runs off the real clock (Unix time), so every angler sees the same game time; each part runs at its own pace: day (07:41–18:43) 10 min, sunset (18:43–22:05) 1 min 30 s, night (22:05–04:48) 8 min 20 s, sunrise (04:48–07:41) 1 min 30 s — so the clock races through dawn and dusk. There is a clock in the menu and HUD: night → dawn → morning → noon → afternoon → evening → dusk → night. Keyframes in `src/daycycle.js` hold the sky gradient, sun/moon glow, cloud colours, star visibility, glint colours and light multipliers for land, water and the foreground; everything between them is blended smoothly. The sun rises on the right, peaks near the top and sets on the left; the moon and stars take over at night, with a pale moon path on the water. No two evenings look alike: each day's sunset takes one of six moods (golden, crimson, raspberry, fiery, lilac, pale) and each dawn one of five (peach, rose, crimson, gold, lilac), picked by the day's number so everyone sees the same sky (`MOODS` and `skyMoodsAt` in `src/daycycle.js`; `?sunset=crimson` / `?dawn=rose` pin one).

The moon has phases (`src/moon.js`): a lunar month lasts eight game days (about 2 h 50 min of real time), off the real clock like everything else, so every angler sees the same moon. The disc in the sky is lit from the right as it waxes and from the left as it wanes, its dark part a faint ghost, and a thin moon lights the clouds and the water less; at night the clock shows the moon in its phase (its phase name on hover). The moon moves the bite (`moonBite`): under a full moon nights bite up to 1.25 times as often and days about 0.94; at the new moon nights drop to 0.85 and days rise to 1.08. `?moon=full` (or `new`, `firstQuarter`, ...) pins a phase.

The sky texture, water palette and glint path refresh four times a second (≈1.3 ms); land and glyph lighting is a multiplier in the WebGL shaders, so the cycle costs almost nothing per frame. URL options: `?time=0.5` pins the clock (0 midnight, 0.25 dawn, 0.5 noon, 0.76 evening), `?day=60` squeezes the whole cycle into 60 seconds, keeping the phase proportions.

Time of day matters for fishing: every species has its own feeding hours (`active` in `src/fishing/data.js`) — catfish at night, pike at dawn and dusk, perch by day — which weight who bites and how soon. The float is drawn smaller the farther out it lies, as the water's glyphs are (full size close in, down to half far out, never under 7 px). At night the plain float is barely visible; the glowing float (shop → Різне) shines through the dark — only then: it stays a plain float by day, starts to glow as dusk falls and shines fully at night. It is drawn with the plain float, in the same layer, so the rod, the reeds and our bank still pass in front of it (it was once drawn last, over everything).

## Weather and wildlife

- **Rain** (`src/weather.js`) follows real time, like the clock, so every player sees the same weather: time is cut into 12-minute slots and a hash of each slot decides whether it brings a shower (2 in 5 do), when, how long (1–2.5 minutes) and how heavy. Showers fade in and out over ~12 s; it rains about 7% of the time. It brings slanted streaks, splashes on the water, a heavier cloud cover and a dimmer scene, with a rain cloud beside the clock. About a third of the showers are **thunderstorms**: heavier and darker, with a lightning strike every 6–20 s (a jagged bolt from the top of the sky down to the far treeline, the whole scene flashing bluish with a flicker) and thunder after it, a sharp crack and rumble when near, a late low roll when far. The strikes are hashed from the slot too, so everyone sees the same flash. Fish bite much faster in the rain (about twice as fast in a heavy shower). `?rain=1` forces rain, `&storm=1` adds a strike every 8 s.
- **Wind** (`src/wind.js`) follows real time too: 8-minute slots, each with its own strength and direction from a hash, blended into the next over the last fifth of the slot; half are calm (0.05–0.25), a third a breeze (0.3–0.6), one in six strong (0.65–1), and rain adds to it. It sways the trees harder and leans them downwind, bows the reeds, bends the grass and the meadows' gust bands its way, carries chimney smoke flatter and farther, slants the rain and drifts the snow, and ruffles the water (`config.waveAmplitude` and `config.ruffle`). Past 0.45 a gust icon shows by the clock (with its name on hover), and gusts whoosh past now and then, panned the way the wind blows. For the fishing: the bait lands a little downwind of where you tapped (up to about 5% of the screen on a long cast in a strong wind), past 0.6 it slowly pushes the float along even on still water, and `windBite` livens the bite up to 1.1 in a light ripple (around 0.45) and slows it to 0.85 in a gale. `?wind=0.8` pins the strength.
- **Ducks** (`src/wildlife.js`): every minute or two, one to three ducks (by day only) paddle in from just off-screen — a side, at the height of the near water, or the bottom edge, never where a bank or cliff meets the edge — keep to the near water not far from the angler for two or three short stretches (`nearZone`: the near half of the water, across the middle three quarters of what's on screen; each stretch at most about a third of the view from the last) and leave the way they came, steering around banks, rocks and the crowns of trees leaning out over the river, with a wake behind them. A duck that swims over the float eats the bait (the hook stays): "Качка з'їла наживку". Reeling in early saves it.
- **More wildlife** (`Critters` in `src/wildlife.js`, drawn by `drawCritters`), for the look and sound only: every few minutes by day a grey heron (in fine 14 px glyphs: a body shaded pale to dark with the black shoulder patch, a long S of a neck streaked down its front that uncoils forward and down on a stab, a black crest, a yellow beak, long legs; in flight the neck drawn in, legs trailing and broad black-tipped wings beating through three positions) glides in from a side on slow wingbeats, lands in the shallows clear of the reeds, stands half a minute or more stabbing at the water every 5–14 s (a splash and rings; one stab in three comes up with a wriggling fish) and flies off past the other side, with its croaking call now and then; in the evening (18:00–22:30) at every water but the castle lake a beaver swims across with a long V of a wake and may slap its tail and dive (a smack, a gulp and rings); on warm days (spring and summer, 9:00–18:30, no rain, no strong wind) up to three dragonflies hover by the reeds in the bottom corners and dart from place to place.

Both are explained in the handbook.

## Fishing

The game opens on a **tiled main menu** on a plain dark ground of faint glyphs. On wide screens it is a grid that fills the screen and scales with it: the clock and money up top; a big tile with the fishing spots, each spot's picture (a shot of the water itself at 17:00, in the season the scene is in, without the rod: `images/spots/<spot>-<season>.webp`, 1920×1056, taken from the game at 960×528 with no rain and cut to fit the tile; every shown water's shot for the season is fetched ahead, so turning to another water shows it at once; the painted picture stays under it while it loads and for a water with no shot yet), a line of plain facts (type, depth, bottom, current), a mark for each water under it to jump straight to one, and **Рибалити** as wide as the picture; beside it a profile tile (the angler's picture, name, rank and level with a bar), the tournament tile (the week's fish, time left and your best, then the top three each in a cell with place, picture, name and weight) and one wide tile for today (the quest, the fish of the day, days in a row); along the bottom the sections. On phones the same tiles stand in one column and the sections sit in a bar fixed to the bottom. A phone on its side (landscape, under 500 px high) gets its own layout: the water, its picture filling the height, and **Рибалити** on the left, staying put; the profile, today and the tournament on the right, scrolling; the sections in a slim bar along the bottom, icon and name side by side; all clear of the notch. On a phone either way up, a page keeps its heading (title, money) fixed at the top while it scrolls under it, the title smaller; **Назад** becomes an arrow in a strip of its own along the bottom, under the thumb, and the page ends with room for it, so it never lies over the page's own buttons; in the shop it sits beside the basket's bar instead, the two plain blocks of one height (what is in the basket, Pay) lined up with the list. Every section opens as a page of its own with **← Назад** (Esc too): Today, the tournament, the profile (picture, account and level with every rank), trophies, collection, shop, backpack, settings; only the moments of fishing (the catch card, the keepnet, the bait picker) stay windows. Each thing lives in one place: the tournament only on its page, the ranks only on the profile. Spacing keeps one rule throughout, inner never more than outer. The sections: **Магазин** (a list: each tab's wares in rows, two columns on wide screens, each with its picture, name, what it does under it (how heavy a fish a hook or a rod holds, what an extra is for; bait needs no words) and how many are in the backpack, and a price tag on the right with what a tap adds and for how much. A tap puts a ware in the **basket**: bait and hooks by the ten (the tag shows the price of ten), line, lures and groundbait one at a time, a rod or an extra once (a second tap takes it out); a gold tag on the row says how many are in. The basket bar sticks to the bottom of the shop: how many things and **Оплатити** with the sum, or how much is short. Tapped, it opens the basket's own page over the shop (**Кошик**, with **← Назад**), with − and + by the same steps and **Прибрати** for one of a kind, and the same bar to pay. Paying takes the money for everything at once, puts it all in the backpack and goes back to the shop; the basket lives for the visit only), **Рюкзак** (laid open in pockets: a rod case, a tackle box with hooks, line and lures, the bait and a pocket for the rest, the hooks counted in the box itself; a tap takes up a rod, hook or bait at once and slides open a drawer under its line saying what it is, with its other action: a new line, baiting the water, reading) and **Трофеї**. The in-game HUD is kept bare: the clock top left (with the weather, and a square groundbait chip beside it while the water is baited), the door icon (back to the menu) top right, the bait in use and how much is left bottom left (tap it to pick another bait), and the backpack bottom right (rods, hooks, bait and extras). Arriving at the water keeps the bait put on last time while any is left (a lure while the spinning reel is there too), otherwise puts on the most expensive bait you own (bread if nothing else).

- **Spots** decide which fish live there, how often anything bites and how big it grows, in three steps (`minKg`, `typicalKg`, `maxKg` with `bigChance`, `tailKg` with `tailChance` in `LOCATIONS`; bait and cast distance scale the odds of a big and a rare one; a fish stays within its own kind's weights (save one bite in about 400, `OVERSIZE`, where a kind grows to its full size: then it outgrows it by up to a tenth), and a kind that never grows to the water's `minKg` keeps its whole range, only rare; and a kind that reaches its own limit at a water, a rudd at the lake, has the top 15% of its range as its big step there, leaning light, so the heaviest possible fish stays rare instead of every big one landing at exactly that weight, as all of them once did). The pond holds only small fry, mostly up to 0.5 kg, a crucian up to 1.5; the lake mostly up to 2 kg, often 2–4, very rarely up to 6; the castle lake has few small fish, mostly 1–4 kg, a pike or zander up to 10 one bite in 40; Pripyat big fish, mostly 5–10 kg, very rarely up to 12, the small ones seldom; the paid old bridge only big ones, predators from 800 g, catfish mostly up to 12 kg, often up to 40, very rarely up to 60 (catfish grow to 70, kept for a water of giants still to come). Nothing in the game says which fish lives where — only the **Довідник рибака** (an extra) does.
- **Opening the waters** (`unlock` in `LOCATIONS`, `src/fishing/waters.js`): the pond is open to everyone; the lake opens at the third rank (Любитель), the castle lake at the fourth (Рибалка), Pripyat at the fifth (Досвідчений рибалка); the old bridge, the trout farm, the quarry and the carp lake take a membership fee of 15 000, 40 000, 80 000 and 150 000 $, paid once (`progress.waters` holds the paid ones). The same rules for every save: an older save standing at a water it can't open yet goes back to the last open one, and trophies from anywhere stay. A shut water shows dimmed on the spot tile under a note of what opens it, its mark under the picture hollow; at the paid one **Рибалити** becomes **Вступити** with the fee (a first tap asks to confirm for four seconds, a second pays), or says how much is short. A new rank that opens a water says so in a note after the rank's own; the handbook's Waters tab tells how each is entered. The daily quest is always one for the pond (its fish, or a count of fish there).
- **Waters still being tried out** (`preview: true` in `LOCATIONS`; `access`, `spotShown` and `fishShown` in `src/fishing/data.js`): shown only on a tester's account, which is an admin's (`cloud.admin`, set in `main.js` as the account is recognised); a tester also has every water open, ranked and paid alike, to test them. Anyone else never meets such a water, nor the fish, legend, handbook pages and achievements that belong only to it, and the counted achievements (all species, all legends) count only what this angler can meet. A save left at a hidden water goes back to the last open one. None is being tried out now: the last three, below, were tested this way and then opened to all.
- **The paid waters past the bridge**: the trout farm **Смерекове** (a paid water at 40 000 $), in the mountains under pale limestone peaks and spruce woods, fished from a wooden jetty; stocked, so it bites often (pace 2; it was 1.6, until the old bridge out-earned it at every stage), the fish even, from 800 g, mostly up to 3 kg, a big rainbow trout up to 8 one bite in 12: rainbow trout (keen on corn, worm and the spinner, takes the spoon too), brook trout (bloodworm, worm, spinner; dawn and dusk), the castle lake's trout, and now and then a huchen (Red Book, let go: livebait or the spoon and wobbler, at night and dawn). Its legend is Veselka, a rainbow trout of 14–16 kg; its badges, Знавець Смерекового and Хранитель гір (let a huchen go). The **Гранітний кар'єр** (80 000 $), a flooded granite quarry after a photo: sheer jointed walls of granite drawn in glyphs straight up out of the water, rust stains and cracks down them, the woods standing on their rim (`onRim`, see `rimLift` in `src/scene.js`), still water that mirrors it all (`water.mirror`: the far shore painted upside down beneath the water's glyphs, the reflection drawn in level strokes), and our own bank of pale granite slabs (`foreground.slabs`, no grass). Predators only, from 2 kg, mostly up to 8, a big one up to 12 one bite in 6, now and then a pike up to 14: pike, zander, steelhead (new, lures only, dawn and dusk, the dearest by the kilo), chub (new; corn, spinner, wobbler; by day) and humpbacked perch from a kilo on worm or livebait. Its ways (`spotMood` in `src/fishing/data.js`, shown on its handbook page): `deep`, a cast in the near third hardly ever gets a bite, and `twilight`, bites ×1.4 at dawn and dusk and ×0.8 in the middle of the day. Its legend is the Granite pike, 20–24 kg, on the spoon or wobbler; its badge, Знавець кар'єру. And the carp lake **Тиха заводь** (150 000 $): we sit in the low ground between two green hills (a first ridge of round `hills`, with bushes, lone trees and winding paths on their open slopes above the woods along the shore, `hillDecor`), a stone viaduct of seven arches carrying a railway high across between them (`viaduct`, its lamps lit after dark), and now and then a steam train running along it from one edge of the view to the other, the line cut across both hills, in fine glyphs six rows high with a dark outline (`TRAIN_UNITS` in `src/canvas-renderer.js`): a locomotive with its chimney, dome, cab window and red buffer beam, wheels turning and the rod between them going to and fro, steam at the cylinders, a tender heaped with coal, and either four carriages (now and then a red one among the green) or, half the time, a goods train of box wagons, logs, coal and a tank wagon; its smoke rising and drifting off on the wind, its lamp and windows lit at night (`drawTrain`). - **Bait:** bread is free and endless but only tempts tiddlers; everything else is bought per piece. If no local fish takes a bait, nothing bites.
- **Rods:** from a willow switch (0.6 kg, no reel) to a catfish rod (no weight limit: rated 50 kg for the fight, it never gives); each looks different in game. Prices climb steeply — roughly 20 min of play for the second rod, ~13 h for the last.
- **Tackle** (Shop → **Снасті**; `HOOKS`, the lures in `BAITS`, `takesHook` and `straightenChance` in `src/fishing/data.js`): each piece of gear does one job. The **rod** decides how hard a fish is to bring in (how fast the tension bar fills against its rated weight: a fish far under the rating hardly loads it, the bar filling at `0.15 + 0.85 × strain^0.8` of the pace, a quarter at a tenth of the rating, while it comes in just as fast, so a 1 kg fish on the carbon rod uses about a fifth of the bar where it once used nearly all of it; the ladder 32.0 h after) and how heavy a fish it can bring in at all: past its rating the line snaps at a random point of the way in, all but 3% of the time (`rodGiveChance`, `HOLD_LUCK`), costing the hook and the bait; only the catfish rod (`holdsAny`) has no weight limit: it never gives and, with a fast reel (2), brings in a fish of up to its 50 kg with little trouble (a 40 kg grass carp in about 12 s, a 50 kg catfish in about 20 s), while past 50 kg the fight gets harder rather than the line snapping (the bar fills 0.9 faster per rating over instead of 2.5, and each rating over needs 1.5 times as many pulls again: an 80 kg catfish takes about 45 s and gets away from the simulated angler about one time in six), the shop and backpack saying "без обмежень по вазі". The ratings match the waters they are for: willow 0.6, hazel 1.2, bamboo 2, telescope 4, glass 10, carbon 14, carp 25, catfish 50 kg (catfish 40 until fights were eased; telescope, glass, carbon and carp were 3.5, 6, 10 and 18 when an overweight fish only filled the bar faster, and a 20 kg fish came in on the 6 kg glass rod). The **hook** decides who can bite and whether it holds: small (any fish, holds up to 1 kg), medium (from 200 g, holds up to 6 kg, the starting size) and large (only from 1 kg, holds any); the size is picked in the backpack, and running out of one size switches to another on the next cast. Below a hook's minimum the edge is soft (from 70% of it bites fade out) and a 3% chance always remains, so a small fish now and then still manages; past its hold weight it bends straight all but 3% of the time (`straightenChance`; it once rose by only 20% per extra hold-weight, so the spoon did the wobbler's job), at a random point of the way in, costing the hook and the bait. So a large hook on the pond gets about 7% of the bites, and a small one by the old bridge gets bent. **Lures** work the same way and add a style: the spinner (150 $; any fish, holds 2 kg, mostly perch, takes even on a steady retrieve), the spoon (300 $; from 300 g, holds 8 kg, best in jerks) and the wobbler (900 $; from 1 kg, holds any, hit in the pause). Each looks like itself on the line and in the water (`drawLureInAir` / `drawLureInWater` in `src/canvas-renderer.js`): the spinner's blade turns round in front of a red bead and buzzes in as a blur of flashes with a line of bubbles; the wobbler is a little green fish with a yellow belly that rocks side to side as it swims and hangs still under the surface in a pause; the spoon is a flashing blade. Hooks cost 4 / 6 / 12 $. Simulated climb of the rod ladder with the best tackle at each stage: about 31.6 h (31.0 h before tackle); with the medium hook only, 38.7 h, because a big catfish bends it.
- **Extras:** landing net (calmer fish near the bank), braided line (slower tension), sonar (faster bites), handbook.
- **Bite:** every bite has a temper — a greedy one grabs almost at once, a nibbler plays a little, a wary one fiddles for a long time, a teaser goes quiet for a few seconds and comes back — and each species leans its own way (`NIBBLE` in `src/fishing/data.js`): perch and pike mostly grab within a second, crucian, tench, bream and carp fiddle for 7–9 s, catfish give few, heavy pulls after long pauses, zander bite gingerly and let go quickly. Twitches, their strength, the pauses and up to two dips differ each time, and each tug shifts the float a little in the fish's own wandering direction; once it takes, the fish drags the sunk float away. On still water the float otherwise stays exactly where it landed; at Pripyat and at the old bridge the current carries it along. Pull on a twitch and the fish is gone with the bait; a pull on a dip hooks it half the time; once the float sinks you have roughly 0.8–1.9 s to strike. The wait before a nibble varies too, with the odd long lull.
- **Keepnet** (`progress.keepnet`, `openKeepnet` in `src/fishing/ui.js`): a landed fish just shows its picture, name, weight and what it's worth, with **Ок**, and goes into the keepnet (a one-of-a-kind fish goes straight to the trophies, an old boot is left on the bank). A chip by the bait, bottom left, shows how many fish are in it; tap it to look. Leaving the water opens the keepnet: every fish with its weight and price, each can be kept as a trophy (for the usual mounting fee) or let go, and **Продати все** sells the rest at once; **Залишити в садку** keeps them for later. The keepnet is saved with the progress, so fish left in it when the game was closed come up again on the next start. A trophy on the player's own wall can be taken down (a small **×** in the corner of its board, which asks **Зняти?** on the first tap, a second within four seconds confirms; `removeTrophy` in `ui.js`): it leaves the save and, for a signed-in angler, its row in the shared `trophies` table (`deleteTrophy` in `cloud.js`, allowed by the table's own-rows delete policy); the mounting fee is not returned.
- **Reeling in:** an empty line (a recast, a fish that got away with the bait, a duck that ate it, a spoon back at the bank) doesn't vanish: the float or spoon skims back across the water toward the bank with a small wake, the rod loading a little, then the rod lifts it out and it swings up to hang from the tip (about 0.9 s from close in to 2 s from far out; `reelIn` / `reelSkim` in `TUNING`). Only then can you cast again. A snapped line has nothing to bring back.
- **A missed bite** (float fishing): if the float goes under and nobody strikes in time, the fish is off with the bait, but nothing says so: the float comes back up and sits still, the line stays out (`bare` in `src/fishing/game.js`), and a fish that comes to the bare hook bites only one time in 200 (`BARE_HOOK_BITE`). Only reeling in tells: "Наживку об'їли", and the bait is gone. A lure that is struck late still just gets away as before. The balance simulator's angler reels in 2–6 s after a missed bite (the ladder 31.8 h, from 31.6).
- **Fight:** hold **Space** or a finger on the water. The tension bar fills at rate X while pulling and drains at 1.5X when released, while the fish swims off at a random pace. A full bar snaps the line. Small fish come in within one full bar; heavy ones need several pulls. The bigger the fish, the wilder the water where it fights (more spray, a wider boil) and a running fish leaves a wake.
- **Catch:** sell, mount as a trophy, or release. Every escape or catch uses one bait (bread excepted).
- **Snags and junk:** reeling in an empty line rarely hooks a snag (≈2% at the pond up to ≈6% at the old bridge) or an old boot (≈1%). A snag won't move and pulling hard snaps the line (hook lost); giving it slack lets the hook work free (only the bait is lost). The boot comes in heavy but doesn't fight, and sells for 2 $ — or makes a joke trophy.
- **Valerka:** a joke — a clownfish that has no business in these waters. Any bite, anywhere, on any bait, has a 1 in 25,000 chance to be Valerka (about once in 190 hours of fishing). It has no price and can't be released: the only choice is to keep it as a free trophy. The handbook never mentions it.
- **More fish:** bleak (tiny shoals at the surface, quick bites; pond, lake, bridge), rotan (greedy: takes any bait, even a lure, via `greedy`; pond), goby (Pripyat), ide (bridge, Pripyat), asp (a lure-only predator striking at the surface; bridge, Pripyat), trout (the castle lake's own fish, dearest by the kilo; spinner or worm), burbot (only on autumn nights: `season: "autumn"` keeps it out of the bites otherwise; bridge, castle lake), grass carp (lake, Pripyat) and silver carp (a Pripyat giant that hardly ever takes a hook). The **sterlet** is in the Red Book (`protected`): it bites rarely by the old bridge, can't be sold, is let go at once and counts towards a badge shown above the trophy wall (`progress.released`). With them the simulated climb of the rod ladder is about 34 h (31.6 before); the old bridge bites a little faster (pace 0.95) so its catfish still turn up as often among the newcomers.
- **Two-headed perch:** the third joke, like Valerka and Artem, but only at Pripyat: a 1 in 25,000 chance, a free trophy only, drawn from the perch's own portrait with a second head.
- **Artem:** the second joke — a bright red crayfish in black sunglasses who simply grabbed the hook. Like Valerka: any bite, anywhere, on any bait, has its own 1 in 25,000 chance to be Artem; no price, can't be released, only a free trophy, and the handbook never mentions him. He has his own pixel sprite (`artemGrid` in `src/fishing/sprites.js`) and drags the bait off slowly, like a crayfish.
- **Economy:** rods cost 300 / 1,500 / 5,000 / 14,000 / 38,000 / 85,000 / 220,000 $; extras: handbook and glowing float 900, spinning reel 2,500 (spoon 300), landing net 7,500, braid 12,000, fish finder 20,000 $; groundbait 130–180 $. Also a bite bell 3,000 $ (rings at every twitch and dip and hard when the float goes under, the phone buzzes, and the strike window is a quarter longer, `BELL_WINDOW`), a feeder 6,000 $ (each float cast feeds the spot it lands on; casting within 0.07 of it again (a patch that looks smaller far out, down to 0.6 of that, `feedRadius`) inside three minutes feeds it one step more, up to five, each step +8% to the bite there, `FEEDER`) and a live bait bucket 4,000 $ (bleak, roach, rudd, crucian, rotan and gobies up to 150 g go into it as live bait instead of the keepnet, up to 20, `LIVEBAIT`). Simulated, the bell adds about 2–17% an hour, the feeder 13–21% on one spot, the fish finder 15–32%. A fish sells for its price per kilo times its weight, tapering for heavy fish (weight^0.88), so a 40 kg catfish is worth a lot but not forty crucians per kilo; a big one (the heavy end of its water, `big`) fetches 1.3 times that and one past its kind's limit 1.6 times (`sizeBonus` in `src/fishing/data.js`), so a rare specimen is worth the hunt. The price per kilo follows how rarely a kind bites: the rare ones (`RARE_FISH`: tench 60, zander 70, asp 70, trout 90, char 95, channel catfish 75, steelhead 110, burbot 85, silver carp 90 $/kg, and pike) are worth 1–2.9 times the average fish of their water, the common ones are cheaper (carp 40, perch 35, bream 30, rainbow trout 65; they were 45, 40, 35 and 80, while the silver carp, the rarest, was the cheapest of the big fish at 35, and the char, biting only at dawn and dusk, cheaper than the rainbow trout); the ladder 28.8 h after (31.8 before). Only a rare fish can be the fish of the day. **Groundbait** has a shelf of its own in the shop, three kinds (`GROUNDBAITS`): basic (120 $; bites ×1.35, peaceful fish ×2), predator (200 $; ×1.35, predators ×3) and premium (3 000 $; ×1.9, the rare fish ×2.5, never given as a gift); the Quiet Backwater multiplies what groundbait adds by 2.2 (`groundbaitPull`, see `groundbaitBoost`). They replace the four bags made for a kind of fish (each ×1.7, its fish ×3; the carplake ×2.5): older saves' carp, bream and trout bags become basic ones, the predator bags stay (`OLD_GROUNDBAITS`). Bites come only as often as the local fish want the bait: a bait nobody there favours goes almost silent, so cheap bait can't be farmed at waters where it doesn't belong. Simulated with realistic play (about 70% of bites struck, heavy fish escaping light rods), the best strategy at each stage is the proper bait for the water (or the spoon, which pays best in the middle of the climb at the castle lake and Pripyat), and each rod takes longer to earn than the last — about 0.4, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 4–5, 8 and 13 hours: some 30 hours of fishing for the full set, before the extras.
- **Hooks:** bought per piece (5 to start); no hook, no cast. A big fish that snaps the line takes the hook with it.
- **Splashes** (`maybeJumpFish` and `nearSchool` in `src/main.js`): a school of fish keeps to one place on the water for 70–160 seconds, then moves somewhere else. It splashes only every 14–36 seconds, bigger, sometimes two in a row, with the splash panned to where it is; stray fish jump anywhere every 12–32 seconds. A cast landing within reach of the school (an ellipse about 7% of the screen height across) bites 1.3 times as often for that cast (`TUNING.schoolBoost`), with no message: the angler has to read the water.
- **Line wear** (`LINE` and `lineWeakness` in `src/fishing/data.js`): every fight wears the line on the reel, by the tension on it each second, more for a heavy fish (weight^0.8), half as fast on braid, and a snap takes 2% at once. `progress.line` is what is left (shown in percent in the backpack, red below 25%); below 25% the tension bar fills up to 1.5 times as fast. Spare lines (Shop → Снасті, 100 $, `progress.lines`) go on by themselves before the next cast once the old line has worn through, or with a tap in the backpack. A line lasts about four hours of small fish early on and under an hour of catfish at the old bridge: well under 2% of what you earn at any stage.
- **Handbook:** a book read a page at a time, after the book in Minecraft: a cream page in a leather cover, **Сторінка N з M** in the corner and arrows at the foot to turn it (the arrow keys and a swipe on a phone too). The first page is the contents with the four sections and their page numbers; each section opens on a list of what is in it: fish, waters and bait as a grid of small pictures with names and page numbers, as many across as the page is wide, the tips as lines with their page after a dotted leader; a tap turns straight to it; then a page for each fish, water, bait and rule. Each page heads with links back to the contents and to its section's list. An entry's page: the picture, the name and its weight and price, then its parts, each under a small red label with a rule running on from it: for a fish, where it lives as a table (the water, how often as a coloured tag, how big), the baits it takes as chips with their pictures, the parts of the day as a strip of six with the ones it feeds best in filled, and how it bites; for a water, its fish as chips and the rest as lines; for a bait, the fish that take it as chips. Nothing in it scrolls: the page is as tall as the screen leaves (on a phone, between the heading and the arrow's strip), and the book is laid out to fit it when it opens and again when the screen changes size: a list takes as many as fit, an entry or a rule too long for one page runs on to the next, sentence by sentence (`bookPages` in `ui.js`, which measures each entry once). On a phone on its side the arrow back moves into the heading, the page turns with arrows at its sides and an entry's picture stands to the left of its text. In the backpack the handbook has its own place at the top, above the rod case, with **Читати ›**. The fish come in four sections by how big a kind grows (`FISH_SIZES` in `ui.js`): **Дрібна риба** (up to 1 kg), **Середня риба** (1–5 kg), **Велика риба** (5–20 kg) and **Гіганти** (over 20 kg), listed in the contents under **Риба**, a little in. The sections: **Риба**: each fish's weight range and price, the waters it lives in (how common it is there and how big it grows), favourite and other baits, feeding times and how it bites. **Водойми**: each spot with its shot (the same as the menu's, in the season the scene is in; in the section's list in bigger cells than the rest, the shot as wide as the cell with the name and page under it), water type, depth, bottom and current, its fish, size limit, odds of a big one, bite pace and snags. **Наживка**: each bait's price, who loves it and who also takes it, and whether it tempts small or big fish. **Поради**: everything that affects fishing. The first three are built from the catalog, so new fish, spots and baits appear by themselves; the tips are kept current with each change.
- **Bait:** 15 kinds, from free bread and cheap dough (small fish only) through worm, maggot, pearl barley, bloodworm, potato, peas and corn to chicken liver, fish strip, boilies, live bait, crayfish and frog for the big ones.

The catalog (fish, bait, rods, extras, spots) lives in `src/fishing/data.js`; fight balance in `src/fishing/game.js` (`fightRates`). Progress is saved in localStorage. Add `?debug` to the URL to expose the game as `window.doberega`.

## Bait and the bite through the day

Thirteen baits: bread, dough, worm, maggot, pearl barley, bloodworm, peas, corn, boilies, live bait, crayfish, frog, and the spoon (potato, chicken liver and fish strip were retired; a save holding any is paid back in full, `RETIRED_BAITS`). Pike and zander are lure fish (`lure` in `FISH`): they take the spoon and almost never a float bait (`FLOAT_PREDATOR`); perch takes both. The handbook lists simply what each fish bites on. Feeding times are set so every water pays about as well by day as by night (catfish keep the night a little better at the old bridge); `balance` scripts compare income per minute by hour and spot.

## Casting, groundbait and spinning

- **Perspective on the water** (`scaleAt` on the game, the ducks and the critters, set in `src/main.js` from the renderer's `waterScaleAt`, 1 at the nearest cast and about 0.5 at the far edge): everything that moves on the water goes by how big the water looks where it is. A hooked fish, a reeled spoon and an empty float skimming in cover equal stretches of water in equal time (`along` in `src/fishing/game.js`), so far out they creep on screen and speed up as they near the bank; a fish's run to the side looks narrower and slower far out; the float's nibble tugs, the pull of a fish that took the bait and the drift on a current shrink with the distance, as do the float's own bob, the boil and spray of a fighting fish, its wake, the lures in the water, splashes, the ducks' and the beaver's pace and the beaver's wake. The school of jumping fish and the fed patch are the same size on the water, so they look smaller far out (down to 0.6 of their size by the bank, to stay hittable).
- **Fish fight back** (`FIGHT` and `fightVigor` in `src/fishing/data.js`, the surges in `updateFight` in `src/fishing/game.js`): each kind has a spirit, from the bream, ruffe and bleak that come in like a wet sack (0.1–0.15) to the carp, grass carp, asp, steelhead and huchen (1.3–1.5), a legend 1.3 times its kind's; times the fish's heft (`√kg / 2`, 0.1 to 1.3, at most 1.8 in all; 1.6 and 2 before the fights were eased) it is the fish's vigor. A fish with vigor over 0.15 often bolts the moment it is hooked (a chance of half its vigor, at most 80%, 0.3–1 s in), then surges off now and then (0.06 per second times its vigor, 0.6–2.1 s each; 0.08 and 0.8–3 s before): it takes line even against the pull (at up to 0.9 of the reeling speed, less on a rod rated well above the fish: `0.3 + 0.7 × strain` of it), held tight it takes only 0.35 of that (`TUNING.runHeld`) and veers off at half the pace, let go it runs at 1.25 of it (`runSlack`) on top of its usual swimming off, so pulling holds it and slack costs line (fights about a tenth shorter, the ladder 31.6 h); it loads the rod on top of the usual strain, veers off to a side (`side`, up to 0.17 of the view out by the bank and less far out, see Perspective below, never onto a bank) and drifts back in line as it tires; while pulled it shakes its head, little spikes on the tension bar. A 10 kg carp on the carp rod makes about 1.6 runs in a 10 s fight, on the carbon rod nearer its limit 5 in 40 s; a 2 kg bream none. The ladder 36.9 h after (32.0 before), so its check now allows up to 50 h. Since fish over 5 kg dragged on, the fights were eased: a fish at its rod's rating takes 3.8 full bars of pulling instead of 5.2, so for the simulated angler a 5 kg pike on the glass rod takes about 6 s (11.5 before), an 8 kg carp on the carbon rod 10 s (21), a 20 kg grass carp on the carp rod 23 s (53); the ladder 31.9 h after (36.9 before).
- **Cast range** (`castDistance`, `castReach`, `withinReach` in `src/main.js`): the limit is a wide arc round the angler (the bottom middle of the view) fitted to the screen (`CAST_ARC`): an ellipse whose top lies 0.14 of the water below the far bank and whose sides are 1.15 times half the visible width out, so it runs off the screen's edges about three quarters of the way down on a wide screen and on a phone alike (it was a circle measured with the water's perspective, which reached hardly any farther to the sides than straight out); a tap on the water beyond it (not on the sky or a bank) lands as far as the rod throws in that direction, and how far out (`reach`, for the odds of a big fish and the cast style) counts along that radius too.
- **Casting** (`castSwing` in `src/canvas-renderer.js`, `castStyle` in `src/fishing/game.js`): close to the bank the rod is swung underhand, dipping to the water and sweeping up, and the bait flies low; far out it goes overhead, up and back over the shoulder, then whips forward, and the bait flies on a high arc.
- **Groundbait** (`GROUNDBAIT`, `GROUNDBAITS` and `groundbaitBoost` in `src/fishing/data.js`): sold by the bag in Shop → Groundbait, a shelf of its own: basic (120 $; bites ×1.35, the peaceful fish ×2), predator (200 $; ×1.35, the predators ×3) and premium (3 000 $; ×1.9, the rare fish, `RARE_FISH`, ×2.5). Its fish are weighted by its `favor` both in who bites and in how keen the bite is (`candidates`, `biteActivity` and `rollFish` take the bag as `favor`); kept in `progress.groundbaits` by kind, the kind in at a water in `progress.baitedKind`, each in its own coloured sack in the shop, the backpack and the chip by the clock. Older saves' bags (carp, bream, trout, predator, and before them the plain one) become basic or predator ones when they load (`OLD_GROUNDBAITS`, `normalizeProgress`). Gifts (days in a row, ranks) are the basic or the predator one, whichever has fish in a water the angler may fish, never the premium (`gift: false`; `giveGroundbaits` in `waters.js`); the tournament gives the one for the week's fish (`groundbaitFor`). At the water, tap it in the backpack: it works for exactly 24 game hours (about 21 real minutes). Game time follows the real clock, so `progress.baited` keeps, by spot, the moment it wears off: it runs out on time whether the game is open, in the background or closed (older saves holding hours left are converted on load). A square chip by the clock shows the time left as a ring round the bag, lit clockwise from the top.
- **Spinning** (`retrieving` phase in `src/fishing/game.js`): a lure (Shop → Снасті: spinner, spoon or wobbler, each with its own `retrieve` weights for jerks, the flutter in a pause and a steady retrieve) is picked like any bait but needs a spinning reel. After the cast, holding reels it toward the water's edge below the rod; short jerks, and the flutter in the pause after one, tempt a strike far more than a steady retrieve. Untouched at the bank, it's cast again; a strike is a bite like on the float. The spoon isn't used up and needs no hooks; only a broken line loses it.

## Daily quest

One quest a day, the same for every angler (`src/fishing/quests.js`): days are counted in UTC, and a hash of the day's number picks a template and its numbers: catch so many of a common fish, one fish of at least a given weight, so many fish at one water, so many kilos in all, or so many kinds. Each can be done with the starting kit at the first waters. It shows as a line above the menu buttons (a notice that gets a green tick once done; the game screen stays bare), and a window with the goal, a progress bar, the reward and the time until the next quest. Done, it pays at once, by the best rod owned: 200 $ with the willow switch up to 5,500 $ with the catfish rod, about a quarter of an hour of fishing at that stage. `progress.quest` keeps today's run at it.

**Fish of the day:** a hash of the same day's number also picks one species (any fish sold at the market but the burbot and the silver carp), shown on a second line of the quest notice. Caught that day it is marked `dayFish` and sells for 1.5 times its price (`DAY_FISH_BONUS` in `data.js`), keeping that price in the keepnet; mounting it as a trophy costs the usual.

**Days in a row:** the first fish (not junk) landed on a day counts it (`progress.streak`: `{ day, count, best }`); a day missed starts the run again. Each counted day brings a gift, round a week (`STREAK_GIFTS`): 10 worms, 5 medium hooks, a groundbait, a quarter of the quest reward, a spare line, two groundbaits, and on the seventh day the whole quest reward. The quest notice shows the run (gold once today counts); its window, now **Сьогодні**, shows the quest, the fish of the day and the week's seven gifts. Two achievements follow it: 7 and 30 days in a row.

Nobody else's progress is listed: the quest is between the angler and the water (the old `quest_done` table is dropped by [`supabase/schema.sql`](supabase/schema.sql)).

## Balance check

`node tools/balance/sim.mjs` (or `--quick` for a rougher run in under a minute, or `--spot <id>` for what one water pays with each rod) climbs the rod ladder with the game's own fishing code: a simulated angler that misses about one bite in five, reacts late, pumps the rod in the fight and fishes lures in jerks and pauses, with the real clock, rain and moon. Waters open as in the game: by rank as its experience comes in, the old bridge once its fee is paid (bought in order of price among the rods). At each stage it screens every open water, bait and hook, measures the five best and fishes the best until the next purchase or opening, paying for bait, lost hooks and lures, worn-out lines and the spinning reel. It prints the best setup, earnings per hour, fish, losses and lines per hour, the hours of each stage and the experience, then checks that the whole ladder takes 22–50 hours (34 until fish began to fight on the line), the second rod under an hour and each rod longer than the last, exiting with code 1 if not. This angler is a little better than the earlier estimate's: about 26 hours for the full ladder before line wear and the moon, 27–28 after, 30.8 once the waters opened one by one, and 25.7 since each water has its own sizes (the lake at 1.6 h, the castle lake at 3.6 h, Pripyat at 6.2 h and paying best until the carbon rod, the old bridge bought at 7.9 h and best from the carbon rod on), and 28.3 with the trout farm on the ladder (bought at 13.5 h, best with the carp rod at about 19 000 $ an hour, then the old bridge again with the catfish rod), and 28.4 with the granite quarry as well (bought at 17.7 h, best from then on at 25 000–30 000 $ an hour), and 33.1 with the carp lake too (33.2 once the capped sizes were spread out, 31.5 once tackle held only up to its rating and the rods were rated for their waters, the wobbler then paying best from the glass rod on; 32.9 with the wobbler's strikes cut by about a sixth and the trout farm biting faster, so each stage has its own best: the spoon at the castle lake, boilies or the wobbler at Pripyat, the wobbler by the old bridge, the trout farm with the carbon rod, the wobbler at the quarry, boilies at the carp lake; bought at 26.1 h; without groundbait, which the simulator leaves out, it pays about as well as the quarry, 30 000–40 000 $ an hour, and with it, ×2.5 there, clearly better). The simulator counts any water still being tried out as if it were open to all.

## Collection

**Колекція** in the main menu has two tabs. **Атлас** shows every fish in the game as a card (built from `handbookEntries`, so a new species appears by itself): a caught one in colour with how many and the heaviest, the rest a dark silhouette marked "not caught yet", with no hint of where or when: finding out is the point (the handbook still tells it). **Ачівки** lists the achievements below. ## Pages opening quickly

A page's first opening makes the browser work out the look of all it holds, some 20 to 100 ms. So a few seconds after the game loads (`ui.warmPages`, after the fish portraits), each page is opened once out of sight in an idle moment and closed again, silently (`.warming`), only while the menu shows with nothing open; the tournament page gets no board, so the server is not asked. The real first opening is then as quick as any after it.

## Headings font

Headings use the game's own pixel blackletter, **Doberega Gothic** (`fonts/doberega-gothic.woff2`, 3 KB): Ukrainian and English letters, figures and marks, each drawn as rows of pixels in `tools/font/build-gothic.py`, which turns them into the font (fontTools; `tools/font/preview.html` shows every letter). Money is dollars: every price, reward and note is written with `$` after the figure (`formatMoney`, a no-break space between), and the money icon is a gold coin with a dollar struck on it (`coinSprite`).

## Angler's picture

Each player picks one of twelve anglers as their picture (`AVATARS` and `avatarSprite` in `src/fishing/sprites.js`): head and shoulders on a 24-pixel grid, lit from the top left (a lit forehead, a shaded cheek, eyes with whites, brows, nose and ears), each with a hat or hair of their own (a bucket hat, a cap, a ribbed bobble hat, a woven straw hat, a fur ushanka, a dotted headscarf, bare-headed or bald), a skin tone, a jacket with its collar, and some a beard, a moustache or glasses. ## Ranks

Experience (`progress.xp`, `src/fishing/ranks.js`) comes from every fish landed, the square root of its market worth (a roach 2, a crucian 4, a good carp 17, a big catfish 30+; a sterlet 15, a joke fish 100, a legend 300; junk nothing), plus 25 for a species never caught before, 20 for the daily quest, 10 for a day in a row and 150 / 100 / 50 for a tournament podium. Ten ranks, from Новачок to Легенда водойми, at 100, 400, 1,300, 3,000, 7,000, 14,000, 30,000, 50,000 and 80,000: measured with the balance simulator (which now prints experience per hour and at each new rod), about 25 minutes, 1.5, 4, 7, 12, 20, 35, 55 and 80 hours of good fishing (the third, fourth and fifth open the lake, the castle lake and Pripyat). Each new rank brings a gift, a share of the quest reward (½ up to 5×) and from the fourth some groundbait; `progress.rank` is the last rank whose gift was given. A save from before ranks gets its experience worked out from its stats (each species' count at a typical weight, species, unique trophies, quests) and the gifts of every rank reached at once, after the cloud has settled. The rank and level sit on the menu's profile tile, and the **Профіль** page shows the bar to the next rank, how experience is earned and every rank with its gift. Others see it by the name in Trophies → Other anglers and on the tournament board (an `angler_rank` column in both tables; the game still publishes trophies to a table from before it).

## Weekly tournament

From Monday 00:00 to Sunday 23:59 Kyiv time (`tourneyWeek` in `src/fishing/quests.js`) every angler fishes for one species, picked by a hash of the week's number and never the same two weeks running: crucian carp, perch, tench, bream, roach, rudd, pike or zander. The format takes turns by week (`TOURNEY_FORMATS`, `tourneyFormat`): the heaviest single fish every other week, and in between a marathon (the most kilos of the week's fish landed all week) or a night tournament (the heaviest caught between 21:00 and 5:00 game time, `TOURNEY_NIGHT`). The formats and the pike and zander start with the week of 16 November 2026 (`TOURNEY_RULES_FROM`); the weeks before keep the six fish and the heaviest format they were played for. Every fish of that kind landed counts, sold or kept; `progress.tourney` keeps this week's score, how many of the week's fish counted, the last place seen, the last week settled and last week's count. A signed-in angler's new score goes to the `tournament` table through `submit_tournament()` ([`supabase/schema.sql`](supabase/schema.sql)), which takes the week from the server's clock, the nickname from the account and turns away a weight past the species' limit (its kind's top plus a tenth, since a fish can outgrow its kind; up to then a crucian of 1.6 kg was silently turned away) or, in a marathon week (`tournament_format()`), past 400 kg; anyone can read the table, nobody can write it directly. A score made before signing in is sent on signing in. The menu's tournament tile shows the week's fish, its format, the time left, your best and the top three (ties sharing a place there too, as the prizes do); the **Турнір** page shows the fish, the format, the time left, the prizes, the top twenty (ties share a place) and the angler's own place below if further down. The angler's place is looked up after their own score goes up, on coming back to the game and every five minutes while it is open; having slipped down since last time, they are told "Тебе обійшли в турнірі" (`checkTourneyPlace` in `src/main.js`). Last week's winners (all who share the top weight, `tournamentWinners` in `cloud.js`) wear a little cup by their name on the board, the tile, other anglers' trophies and their own profile tile, until the next week ends. The next time a signed-in angler comes in after a week, it is settled once: places 1–3 get 3×, 2× and 1× the quest reward and a gold, silver or bronze cup on a shelf above their trophy wall (`progress.cups`; the weeks before cups came in get theirs once per save on the first sync, from the angler's own rows in the table, `backfillCups` in `src/main.js`, cup only, the prize having been paid then), and everyone who landed at least three of the week's fish (`TOURNEY_GIFT.minFish`) two groundbaits for it; two achievements (top three, a win). The results are trusted from the game, so a determined cheat can still post a fake weight within the limit; such a row can be deleted by hand in the SQL Editor.

## Finds and gulls

Reeling in an empty float line now and then brings something up (`press` in `game.js`): an old boot (1.2%), a bottle (0.6%) or someone's lost lure (0.4%: a spinner, now and then a spoon or, rarely, a wobbler, added to the bait). A bottle holds a note, a legend's rumour or an angler's word, or about one in three times 20–200 $ rolled up (`BOTTLE_MONEY`). By day (6:00–20:00 game time) a gull may snatch a fish under 250 g as it comes off the hook, one in 60 (`GULL`): it never reaches the keepnet. Three achievements: five bottles, a lost lure, and a secret one for the gull.

## Legends

Each water has a legend (`LEGENDS` in `data.js`): the golden crucian at the pond (2.2–3 kg), the grey pike at the quiet lake (11–14 kg, lures only), the princely trout at the castle lake (6–8 kg), Myron the carp at Pripyat (28–35 kg) and Grandpa Panas the catfish at the old bridge (70–85 kg). Each is a giant of a kind that lives there, drawn from its kind's look in colours of its own, and bites like it, harder. A bite at its water on a bait its kind takes (never bread or dough) is the legend once in 1,500 (`LEGEND_CHANCE`), some 20–30 hours of fishing that water the right way; being far past its kind's size, it needs a strong rod and a big hook. Hooked, a toast says something huge is on the line; lost, it names the legend that got away. Landed, it goes straight to the trophies, priceless (it is `unique`, like the joke fish), and so onto the public trophy wall. The atlas lists them under **Легенди**, a rumour each until landed (a joke about the fish, never its water); two achievements go with them (one legend, all five).

## Achievements

Collection has an Achievements tab: 50 of them in seven groups (first steps, collecting every fish of a water, weight, skill, time and weather, numbers and money, and specials, six of them secret until earned). ## Accounts and cloud saves

Signing in is optional: the game is fully playable without an account. Progress then lives only in the browser's localStorage, which can be wiped, and the profile tile on the menu says **Увійти** until an account is signed in (then it shows the nickname, or the email); the full warning is in the account section of the **Профіль** page. After the very first fish the game offers once to sign in, with a warning of what skipping means, and a **Продовжити без входу** option. The account section of the **Профіль** page lets a player sign in or create an account with email and password (Supabase Auth), and **Забули пароль?** emails a reset link that brings them back to the game to set a new password. Once signed in, the window shows who you are (avatar letter, nickname, email) and when progress last reached the cloud; the player can set a **nickname** (3–16 letters, digits, spaces, _ or -) with its own **Зберегти** button, live only while the field differs from what's stored, which turns into **Збережено ✓** with a line saying others now see it; it's kept in the account profile so every device shows it; until one is set, the email is shown instead. Below sit **Готово** (close the window) and **Вийти з акаунта** (sign out), both full-width. While signed in, every save is pushed to the player's row in a `saves` table a few seconds after it happens (and on leaving the page); signing in on another device pulls it back. If the device and the account hold different progress, the game settles it by itself, never asking (`reconcile` in `src/cloud.js`): each device remembers, per account, the save it last had in common with the cloud (`doberega.synced.<user id>` in localStorage), so if only one side has moved on since, that side wins; if both have (or the device never synced), the save with more fish caught wins, the newer on a tie, and the rods, extras and water fees bought only on the losing side come over too, at their price (`mergePurchases` in `src/fishing/store.js`; a bell bought on one device was lost when another device, open in the background with older progress, pushed over it). Before each push the device checks the cloud's `savedAt`: if it moved on elsewhere since they last matched, the two are reconciled first instead of the cloud being overwritten; and coming back to the game after a while away reconciles too. Progress replaced by the cloud's is kept aside on the device under `doberega.backup`. Row-level security means each player can only read and write their own save. Each trophy hangs as anglers hang them: the fish on a dark wooden board (painted planks with their grain, a bevel and two brass screws; a legend's board has a gold trim, `plaque` in `src/fishing/frames.js`), a brass plate screwed to the board with the name and, past a groove, the weight, the water, date and bait in small letters below. Another angler's trophy has a bar above the board with their name large and their rank. **Трофеї** has two tabs, both sorted heaviest first: **Мої**, the player's own wall, and **Інших гравців**, the 60 biggest trophies of other anglers (fish, weight, nickname — or “Рибалка” if none is set, never the email — spot, bait and date), readable by anyone, signed in or not. A signed-in player's trophies are published to a public `trophies` table (each once; a new nickname updates them). Every trophy now also records the bait it was caught on.

The account section has two tabs, **Вхід** and **Реєстрація**, each with one big action button. Fields are labelled above (the label stays while typing), passwords have a Show button, a nickname can be set right at sign-up, "Забули пароль?" opens its own small screen that emails a reset link, and the server's usual errors come back in plain words (wrong email or password, account already exists, too many tries, no connection). Signed in, it shows a lettered badge with the name and email, whether progress is safely in the cloud, the nickname (it saves itself when you leave the field or press Enter) and a small Sign out link.

To switch it on:
1. Create a free project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com).
2. In the project, open **SQL Editor → New query**, paste [`supabase/schema.sql`](supabase/schema.sql) and run it.
3. In **Project Settings → API**, copy the project URL and the `anon` public key into [`src/cloud-config.js`](src/cloud-config.js).
4. Optionally, under **Authentication → Providers → Email**, turn off “Confirm email” to let players sign in straight away; otherwise they confirm their address first.
5. Under **Authentication → URL Configuration**, set the **Site URL** to where the game is hosted and add it (plus `http://localhost:8000/` for local testing) to **Redirect URLs**, so password reset links lead back to the game.

With the two values left empty the game runs exactly as before and the account section says cloud saves aren't set up.

## Stats page

`doberega.site/stats` (`stats/`) shows how the game is doing, for admins only: visitors, pressed **Рибалити**, caught a fish, signed up (a funnel with percentages), returning visitors; all-time accounts, players active in the last 7 days, fish caught and trophies; visitors, sign-ups and catches per day; where visitors came from (tags and sites under one plain name: `ig` and instagram.com as Instagram, `fb` as Facebook, t.me as Telegram; `SOURCES` in `stats/stats.js`) and on what device; the newest accounts (nickname, or the email half hidden), when each was made and when it last played. Then open `/stats` and sign in with that account (already signed in if you are in the game on the same site).

## Phones

On a phone held upright the landscape isn't squeezed: the scene is laid out at least 1.15 times as wide as it is tall and the screen shows a slice of it, centred on what each spot is about (the castle crag at the castle lake, the middle elsewhere); reeds, rocks, lily pads and the rod follow the visible slice. The interface adapts too: the menu drops the title so nothing overlaps, puts a full-width **Рибалити** under the spot card and the four sections in a 2×2 grid of large buttons; dialogs fill the screen and scroll inside, the shop shows three wares to a shelf; everything stays clear of the notch and the home bar. The page is pinned to the screen (no scrolling it under the status bar), pinch and double-tap zoom are off, and a long press (holding to reel in) never selects anything or pops a callout.

**As an app on the home screen.** Hosted over HTTPS (e.g. on Vercel: import the repo, framework "Other", no build command, output directory `.`), the game installs like an app: on iPhone, Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android, Chrome → ⋮ → Install app. It then opens full screen from the perch icon, with no browser bars and the status bar over the scene (`manifest.webmanifest` and the `apple-mobile-web-app-*` tags in `index.html`). For accounts, add the site's address to Supabase → Authentication → URL Configuration (Site URL and Redirect URLs).

When the on-screen keyboard comes up (signing in, a nickname), the window moves into the part of the screen still showing and its fields scroll inside the frame, with the field being typed in brought to the middle; the scene isn't rebuilt for the keyboard (a resize that doesn't change the scene's size is skipped).

## Sound

Every sound is made on the fly with Web Audio (`src/sound.js`, `src/music.js`): there are no sound files. Each one was picked by ear in the **sound lab** (`tools/sound-lab/`, also at `doberega.site/tools/sound-lab/`): candidates for every sound side by side, the ones still to choose on top, the chosen ones (marked “✓ у грі”) folded below. The code names the lab number it came from (e.g. `lab: Д4`).

- **Fishing:** a soft underhand swish or a strong overhead one with line peeling off the spool, timed to the release; the float landing as a clear bubble, a lure as a plunk with bubbles as it sinks; a tiny bubble for a twitch, a muffled dip and pop, a small bubble for the bite; a swirl when a fish hits a lure; a quick swish for the strike; the float reel's dry ratchet and the spinning reel's fine anti-reverse clicks; the drag giving line in bursts as a fish runs; light splashes or a tail flutter for small fish, a tail slap and a gulp for big ones; the rod creaking under a heavy fish near breaking; a twang on a snag and a rustle and bubble when it lets go; water running off a landed fish and a soft thump; a dry crack for a snapped line, a small metal click for a bent hook; a squelch and two sad notes for a boot, little bells for Valerka or Artem; a pop and drops as the empty rig is lifted out; a scatter of plops for groundbait.
- **Interface:** soft wooden taps (“Дерево”): buttons, tabs, picks, leafing through spots, a window opening (two taps up) or closing (down), and a low double knock for “can't”. Coins for a purchase, a cascade of them for a rod or gear, the keepnet lifted dripping out of the water and coins for a sale, three hammer knocks for a trophy.
- **Nature,** sparse and quiet, never a steady hiss: a small wave lapping, a distant fish jumping, four songbirds by day (a trill, a tit, a reed warbler, an oriole), ducks muttering or quacking while on the water and quacking after stealing the bait, tree frogs on evenings and nights at the reedy waters, crickets and a little owl at night, rain as drops over a deep murmur, thunder after each lightning strike in a storm. Each water adds its own: the river at the old bridge swelling and fading, a far cow by the village pond, a distant bell over the castle lake, the wreck's rusty groan and a dosimeter's sparse clicks at Pripyat.
- **Night and mystery:** a glassy chime for the wisps, the boatman's creaking wood, the mavka's far wordless song with an echo, the lantern's creaking handle, a deep hum and a gulp for the eyes, a slow low drift deep in the night and, very rarely, a whisper in the reeds.
- **Music** (`src/music.js`): short calm pieces of about a minute, as in Minecraft not all the time: the first a minute or three after reaching the water, then five to twelve quiet minutes between them; nylon-guitar pieces by day, dusk and dawn, pad-and-piano pieces with an echo at night, never the same twice in a row, half as loud during a fight, fading out on leaving the water. Nine pieces, each with its own character: its key, tempo and metre, how it's accompanied (fingerpicked, strummed with an upright bass, a waltz, harp-like rolls, travis picking with a shaker; at night soft pads, low piano chords or a music box) and what carries the tune (guitar, flute, music box, harmonics, piano, cello). The tune is a two-bar motif played A A B A, written from the piece's scale by a fixed seed, so it's recognisable while the playing differs a little each time.

Sound starts **off** for everyone and is switched on in **Settings → Звук** (remembered in this browser), with two volume sliders under it, **Звуки** (effects and nature, 80% to start) and **Музика** (60%). Every sound was measured (rendered offline, its loudest tenth of a second) and given its own level (`LEVELS` in `src/sound.js`) so the fishing sounds sit around −36…−42 dB, the interface a step below and nature, the places and the night quieter still, rain a soft bed; each music piece likewise has its own `level` so none is louder than the rest. While it's off, or the tab is hidden, the audio sleeps and costs nothing. Browsers allow sound only after a tap or a key, so the game is silent until the first one.

## Rendering

Every character is pushed into a glyph batch (position, glyph, color). WebGL2 draws each batch with a single instanced call from a white glyph atlas tinted on the GPU, so the ~25,000 characters on screen cost a few milliseconds per frame. Static scenery (sky gradient, sun, ridges, forest silhouette, water body) is rasterized once into layer textures; clouds and stars are resampled about 15 times per second. Each batch keeps its own GPU buffers and is only re-uploaded when its contents change; the grass refreshes every other frame; the water, the trees and the meadows at most about 32 times a second (`SLOW_INTERVAL`), the water on one frame and the land on the next, so at 60 frames a second (a cast, a fight) no frame carries both and the line, fish and float keep the full rate (an average 60-frame second about 30% lighter on the CPU); and the hottest loops use a sine table (`fastSin` in `src/noise.js`), the meadow breeze too (about 19,000 stalks a frame, their phases kept on each cell). The sky is drawn without blending and only above the shore, and the land layer only from the highest crest down. Reeds and the rock are drawn on a light 2D overlay. Browsers without WebGL2 fall back to Canvas 2D with the same batches.

**Depth.** Each plane of the scene has its own grid, and its glyphs are sized to it, so the far landscape shows far more detail and the near one reads as near. Hills and mountains are laid on a grid 0.4 of the base cell, the far shore, its woods, the shoreline, the plant at Pripyat and the castle hill with its woods and arch bridge on 0.5 (the hill's crowns over a dark backing, shaded by their own glyphs, and only a quarter of its leaves rustling, as many as before), and the water grows from 0.6 at the horizon to 1.6 at the bottom of the screen, its rows and columns spreading out as the glyphs grow; clouds grow from 0.4 at the horizon to 1.15 overhead. Things that lie on the water (lily pads, the jetty and its boat, river rocks, the wreck) use the water's grid where they lie. The near banks and bank trees grow toward the viewer too and are thinned out to match, so they never run together. The glyph shader scales each glyph by its height on the screen (`perspective` in `src/glyph-backend.js`), and the atlas is drawn sharper, with mipmaps, so the large glyphs stay crisp and the tiny ones clean. Cells on the finer grids answer to the base grid for every lookup by row and column (reflections, the treeline, the canopy). The water, the costliest layer, keeps about the cells it had (within a few per cent at most spots, about 15% more at the pond and the quiet lake); the hills and woods are part of the static layers. `?depth=hills,far,near` tries other sizes, `?depth=off` draws the old even grid.

Keeping it light (a laptop should stay cool): the scene draws at most 60 frames a second (a 120 Hz screen would otherwise do everything twice), and those 60 only while things move fast — a cast, a fight, reeling in, a fresh splash, a finger on the screen, and for a moment after. While you just wait at the water it draws 24 (the water drifts slowly, so it hardly shows), 20 in a window you aren't looking at, and 12 under the menu or an open window, where the scene barely shows. WebGL asks for the low-power (built-in) GPU: the glyphs are light work, and the discrete GPU on a dual-GPU laptop runs hot. The music's level is only re-set when it actually changes (a ramp every frame piled up in the audio thread without end). On a phone the canvas is wider than the screen; only the water on screen is simulated, rain falls only there, and the GPU and the overlay touch only the visible slice (a scissor). The water sample writes into one reused object instead of allocating per cell, still water keeps each cell's wind coordinates, the reflection wobble is one sine per row, and the reeds only change colour when they must. Measured in the browser (before the depth grids), a frame went from about 7 ms to 4 ms on a desktop and from 10 ms to 2.5 ms on a phone-sized screen, with an identical picture. Leafing through spots in the menu only changes the picture there; the scene behind the menu is built when you press **Рибалити** (the button says **Завантаження…** for that moment), and only once (it used to be laid out twice). Grading the land layer is an unrolled, allocation-free loop over the pixels (`gradeImageData` in `src/theme.js`, the same sums as `gradeRgb`): building a scene went from about 0.7–1.4 s to 0.2 s, and a spot switch in the menu from over a second to under 15 ms. Now the land layer isn't read back at all: its grade is a colour matrix in the layer shader (identical to the pixel loop within 3 levels of 255), and the winter snow pass reads only the slice on screen below the highest hill, so a rebuild went from about 220 ms to 70 ms on a desktop at phone size. Each glyph batch takes turns between three sets of GPU buffers, so an upload never waits on the GPU still drawing the last frame. The painted UI frames (window borders, pockets, spot pictures) and the pixel sprites are written into a pixel buffer and put on the canvas once, not one `fillRect` per pixel; fish portraits are shaded once per species and ahead of time while the game is idle, so the handbook's fish tab opens in about 12 ms instead of 70–100 ms. The weather icons by the clock switch with a little slack, so wind or rain hovering at the line doesn't redraw them every frame. 

## Run locally

From this folder, start any static file server. For example, with Python 3:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000> in a modern browser.

## Water settings

The water look is fixed in `src/main.js` (`config`): 5 px spacing, 7 px glyphs, 0.1× wave speed, flat surface (amplitude 0), 51% shimmer. Resizing the viewport rebuilds the character grid.
